Case studies / Marketing measurement

Found email was undercounted about 10x, and open rates predicted nothing

GA4 was crediting email with roughly a tenth of what the email platform attributed, and campaign success was being read from open and click rates. I measured the gap, showed automated flows out-earn broadcasts about 28x per recipient, and proved across 42 sends that open rate had no relationship to revenue.

A B2B industrial equipment company selling by web, phone and dealers, running its email program in Klaviyo with web analytics in GA4. I owned the measurement work and the rules that came out of it. Designed and ran the analysis solo and wrote the resulting selection rules into the email build process.

The problem

GA4 made email look like a minor channel because Klaviyo links did not carry consistent UTM tags, so email traffic landed in Direct. Engagement metrics looked healthy, but nobody had tested whether open or click rates had anything to do with revenue. Both readings were steering where testing effort and send decisions went.

What I built

I pulled 12 months of Klaviyo campaign and flow value reports and compared them with what GA4 credited to email. The gap was about 10x. Splitting the total showed automated flows earning $11,645 per 1,000 recipients against $205 for broadcast campaigns, about 28x per recipient.

I then ran 42 consumer campaign sends through a correlation check. Open rate against revenue came out at −0.008 and click rate at −0.189. Low-earning sends had slightly better engagement than the top earners, and unique clicks stayed between 287 and 361 on every send. What separated winners was whether clicks bought: top sends averaged 2.67 conversions at a $1,140 AOV against 0.50 at $87.

I turned the findings into rules in the email build process: never size email from GA4, put testing effort into flows, and pick featured products on average order value and web revenue instead of open rates. Those went in as four new hard rules.

Decisions that mattered

  • I sized email from the email platform's attributed orders instead of GA4, because missing UTM tags were routing email traffic to Direct.
  • I tested engagement against revenue before accepting it as a success metric, and dropped it when the correlation came back near zero.
  • I moved testing effort to automated flows because they earn about 28x more per recipient than broadcasts.
  • I reported the attribution gap as found but not fixed, since the tagging work was still open.

How the result was checked

Both sides of the gap came from source reports: Klaviyo's campaign and flow value reports over 12 months, and GA4's email channel credit. The correlation used every consumer campaign send in the window, 42 in total. The gap is identified, not closed; consistent UTM tagging was still open.
