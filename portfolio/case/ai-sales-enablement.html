Built an AI product matcher and sales gym from real calls

Reps had to memorize 186 products across 9 categories, learned by shadowing, and built every quote by hand. I built a tested product recommendation engine, an AI practice gym trained on 3,009 real call transcripts, and a quote bot that drafts a quote in Salesforce before each booked call.

A B2B industrial equipment company selling by web, phone and dealers, with a small sales team and a catalog spread across two WooCommerce stores. I designed and built all three tools. Designed and built all three tools solo, including the test suite and the training data preparation.

The problem

Choosing the right scale meant knowing capacity, platform size, accuracy and certification requirements across 186 products in 9 categories. New hires had no structured curriculum and no safe place to practice objection handling before real calls. Quotes for booked calls were researched and assembled manually in Salesforce by a senior rep.

What I built

The Sales Matrix turns 3 to 5 qualification questions into 2 to 3 ranked product recommendations. Products pass hard filters on requirements, then a soft ranking scores fit, with a house-brand bonus when products are equally qualified. It covers 186 curated products with structured spec overlays, routes across 9 category modules, disambiguates vague requests, and pulls live WooCommerce pricing from both stores on a 15-minute cache. I backed it with 457 unit tests built from edge cases and real customer scenarios.

The Sales Gym uses Claude to play realistic buyers, built from 3,009 real call transcripts and 9,286 JSONL training entries, at 3 difficulty tiers. Reps drill individual phases of the CLOSER framework until they pass 10 in a row, or run a full call scored on a 22-point rubric, with a leaderboard and mastery tracking.

The quote bot fires when a prospect books a call, finds the lead or contact in Salesforce, pulls any chat qualification data, and has Claude draft a Quote with line items and reasoning. The senior rep reviews every quote first, and each correction is logged as training data for the next prompt.

Decisions that mattered

  • I used hard filters before scoring so a product that fails a requirement can never be recommended, however well it ranks on everything else.
  • I built the practice buyers from real call transcripts instead of generic personas so reps rehearse the objections this market actually raises.
  • I shipped the quote bot as generate-and-review first and held back auto-send, so a person approves every quote while the feedback loop builds.
  • I documented that 7 of 9 categories still needed dedicated training documents rather than presenting the gym as complete.

How the result was checked

The recommendation engine passes 457 unit tests covering boundary conditions and real customer scenarios. The quote bot runs with a human in the loop: every AI-drafted quote is reviewed by the senior rep before the call, and that feedback is logged.
