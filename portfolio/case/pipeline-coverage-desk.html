Covered a senior colleague's $12.0M book without one destructive write

A senior colleague's book of 505 open opportunities worth $12.0M needed covering while they were out, with no written record of how it was worked. I traced the real call list from the phone log and built a coverage desk the same day that could call, log and hand back, and could not change an owner, close a deal or send an email.

A B2B industrial equipment company selling by web, phone and dealers, with the pipeline in Salesforce and calls on RingCentral. I sell by phone and covered a senior colleague's book alongside my own, and it had to go back intact. Traced the call list, built the coverage desk and worked the book by phone.

The problem

The book was large and undocumented: 505 open opportunities worth $12.0M, worked by phone, with nothing written down about where the daily queue came from. Guessing wrong meant spending the coverage period calling the wrong people. Covering someone else's pipeline also carries its own risk: reassigned ownership, changed stages, an automated sequence reaching those customers or a deal wrongly closed would all damage a book that has to be handed back as it was found.

What I built

I traced the call list from the RingCentral call log instead of guessing. Matching all 874 numbers dialed in 30 days against Salesforce, 618 hit both a Lead and a Contact, so the list was converted inbound customers, not a saved view or a cold list. Open tasks weren't the list either, and raw call-task counts were inflated because the phone integration logged calls twice. The real answer was the open opportunity book, worked by phone top to bottom.

The desk turned 489 dialable opportunities into a cadence-driven queue, with about 346 worth $9.4M in the active lane, business-day aware, and the first coverage touch forced to be a call. A hot-signal layer tags every signal by source and date so a computed signal can't pass for a caught reply, and I narrowed hot from 176 of 473 cards to about 49, close to a real day's volume. An already-called scanner reads the account call log every 15 minutes so nobody is dialed twice.

I also checked what the pipeline really held. Line items showed 373 real-SKU scales worth $3.0M closeable against 65 custom jobs worth $7.7M, so 85% of pipeline dollars sat in custom quotes, which changed how the book should be worked. Since most of it was already quoted, I opened fresh territory by ranking 2,426 disqualified leads with a usable phone by how real they looked.

Decisions that mattered

  • I never built Closed Won, Closed Lost, ownership changes or email sends into the desk, because covering someone's pipeline has to leave it exactly as found.
  • I kept the book out of the automated follow-up cadence so those customers never received automated sequences while their rep was away.
  • The already-called flag shows context but never blocks a dial; the rep decides.
  • I scrapped a planned never-sent-quotes tab after finding 100 draft quotes on opportunities already at Proposal Sent or Negotiation, so Draft was not a reliable signal.

How the result was checked

The call list was proven from 874 dialed numbers rather than assumed. Ownership was verified after every write, and the only write, an additive activity task stamped with a coverage note, was tested live and the test record deleted. The already-called scanner's first live run read 1,233 calls across 14 days, and after I corrected how support callbacks were read it produced zero unjustified flags. A quote-number fallback that matched five cards, all to the wrong customer, was tested and left out.
