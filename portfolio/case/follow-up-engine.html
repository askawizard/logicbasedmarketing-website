Case studies / Sales follow-up automation

Every open quote followed up, every quoted deal back in view

Open quotes went cold because nothing chased them, and the rep's call board was hiding most of the quoted pipeline. I built an 11-step follow-up cadence with 8 stop gates and rebuilt the board so every quoted deal renders, including $150.7K of live quotes that had been filtered out.

A B2B industrial equipment company selling by web, phone and dealers, where reps work a book of several hundred open leads and quoted opportunities by phone. I built both systems and am the rep they run for first, as the proving ground before a team rollout. Designed and built the cadence engine and the board rebuild, and work the board myself as the proving-ground rep.

The problem

After a quote went out, follow-up depended on memory, and the risk ran both ways: live deals died of neglect while a naive automation could keep emailing someone who had already bought. The follow-up board itself was misreporting in several ways at once. The Contact Quoted column showed a badge of 129 with 50 cards painted, three board queries carried LIMIT 500 against a book of 402 open opportunities, a support filter was holding back 46 cards, and 31 of 177 cards understated when the customer was last called. Three systems wrote a task for the same call, leaving 749 surplus rows out of 2,459 and inflating the touch count that picks each deal's cadence step.

What I built

The cadence runs 11 short check-ins against open quotes, modeled on a senior rep's real templates with a median around 15 words. A daily run sends what is due, a sweep every 15 minutes flushes closed deals and polls for replies, and any one of 8 gates stops it: closed, stage or owner change, hidden, reply, manual pause or exit, or a sibling opportunity on the same account closing won. It only runs for a configured owner list, and rep clicks on the board go into a durable queue drained by a worker with retries.

On the board I wired in the RingCentral call log so dials made outside the desk show up, and rewrote the support filter so a card is suppressed only when the support touch is newer than the viewing rep's own last work, with quote creation counting as work. I removed the render caps, paged the queries past LIMIT 500, and moved performance into CSS so off-screen cards skip layout and animation.

Then I made the board hold still. Columns rank by deal value with deterministic ties, the board never reloads unless clicked, one matcher builds every date on the card, and touch counts dedupe on the Pacific calendar day because integration lag reached a p90 of 105 minutes.

Decisions that mattered

  • The support filter has to lose to the rep's own work, because one touch on the main line was sweeping live deals off the board for a full 45-day window.
  • Rank by deal value with no recency term, so a card never moves the moment it is worked and the rep keeps his place.
  • Show staleness with a header pill instead of reloading, because every call hands off to the dialer and returning to the tab was triggering a refresh.
  • Gate 8 stops chasing across a whole account, so buying anything ends follow-up on every open quote there.

How the result was checked

Every change was checked on the live board with numbers: badge equals cards in every column (Open Leads 192/192, Working 191/191, Contact Quoted 143/143), 139 cards strictly descending by value, and the touch dedupe A/B'd against the prior build with 0 cards newly overdue. After the call-log wiring the board showed 281 dial badges. Cadence sends are confirmed against the send log, not a health endpoint.
