Mined 3,009 sales calls to find the real large-deal leak

Large equipment deals had slowed while smaller orders kept growing, and every popular explanation sounded plausible. I tested each one against the data, then mined 3,009 recorded sales calls: lost deals raised financing 126 times, far more than price or lead time.

A B2B industrial equipment company selling by web, phone and dealers. I owned the diagnosis end to end: the CRM analysis, the call transcript pipeline and the ranked fix list. Designed and ran the diagnosis solo: built the transcript pipeline, ran the agent analysis and wrote the ranked prescription.

The problem

Large equipment deals had slowed while smaller orders and inbound demand kept growing. Several explanations were circulating, including lead times and follow-up discipline, and loss reasons were blank on all 79 lost opportunities, so nothing in the CRM said why deals were lost. Acting on the wrong cause would have cost months.

What I built

I tested the obvious causes first. Follow-up held up: 81% of opportunities carried activity at 5.8 tasks each, with a 7-day median since last touch. An apparent collapse in win rate turned out to be a measurement artifact in how historical opportunities had been entered, so I rebuilt the comparison on like-for-like records.

With five plausible causes eliminated, I went to the calls. I built a Python extractor on RingCentral's AI transcript API that pulls speaker-attributed transcripts with scores, summaries and next steps, with retry logic and a fallback lookup; the first pull processed 1,523 recorded calls. I then ran four parallel Claude agents across 3,009 transcripts and tallied what came up on lost deals: financing 126 times, price 73, lead time and shipping 51.

The calls showed lead time was a strength and the deciding moment was the budget conversation. I ranked the fixes: a scripted, proactive financing offer on every budget objection first, then a turnkey option, a refurbished path and mandatory loss-reason capture. I also rebuilt the large-deal follow-up board with a classifier that matches on deal name or any line item, cutting it from 67 mixed cards to 43 relevant ones.

Decisions that mattered

  • I treated an apparent collapse in win rate as suspect until the underlying records were checked, because acting on it would have sent the team after a problem that did not exist.
  • I tallied objections across every lost deal instead of reading a handful of calls, so the fix list was ranked by frequency rather than by the most memorable story.
  • I put mandatory loss-reason capture on the fix list because it was the cheapest change and the reason the pattern had stayed invisible.
  • I reported the early results with an explicit caveat instead of claiming a trend from one strong month.

How the result was checked

Each rejected cause was checked against live CRM, search and accounting data before the transcripts were mined. Objection counts were tallied across every lost deal, not a sample, and the early large-deal results after the change were reported as a signal to confirm, not a proven trend.
