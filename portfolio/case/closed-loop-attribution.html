Teaching Google Ads to bid on phone sales, not just web checkouts

Google Ads could only see website purchases, so bidding ignored phone sales, the company's larger channel. I connected closed Salesforce deals back to the ad click, recovered attribution for 111 wins that had none, and raised the revenue Google can train on by 32%.

A B2B industrial equipment company selling by web, phone and dealers. The ad agency ran the Google Ads account; I owned the Salesforce side, the lead capture pipeline and the data that feeds bidding. I found the real blockers, designed and built the Salesforce side (fields, flows, backfills, the reconciler and every guard) and the lead source capture pipeline, and verified the import end to end. The ad agency connected the import inside Google Ads.

The problem

Leads arrived from chat, booking links and phone calls, and none of those paths carried the source into Salesforce, so a closed deal could not be traced to the click that started it. When the Google Ads offline conversion import was attempted, the obvious click-ID field turned out to be the wrong one: web-form click IDs sat on 38 of 671 recent Closed Won deals and phone click IDs on 142 of 671, with an overlap of 2 records. Mapping the obvious field would have thrown away about 80% of the signal. Opportunity field history was also off, and Close Date was a date where Google requires a datetime, so the import stalled at the last step.

What I built

I started at first touch. I wired chat, booking and call-tracking leads into Salesforce with source capture: ad click IDs from both search engines, 7 UTM fields, and referrer inference for organic and direct. Every path shares one dedup order (email, then phone in multiple formats, then Contact) and never overwrites the original source. Ad landing URLs were breaking a 255-character field, so I moved them to a 2,048 character field that carries through lead conversion.

For the import I built one click-ID field that combines web and phone IDs, a writable copy Google's picker can map, before-save flows that stamp it automatically, and a datetime stamped when a deal moves to Closed Won. I backfilled 740 of 740 opportunities with 0 mismatches and backfilled the timestamp for 2,387 won opportunities. A daily reconciler then recovers click IDs that were never recorded by matching won deals to ad calls on the customer's phone number, and a provenance field marks each one recorded or recovered.

Then I guarded against teaching Google the wrong thing. Web purchases never route through Salesforce, 11 opportunities that had already fired the web purchase pixel are suppressed as double counts, and validation against real click times caught 5 timestamps earlier than the click and 16 outside the 90-day window, both now zero.

Decisions that mattered

  • I measured the conversion window instead of assuming one: median lead-to-close is 1 day and 98.4% close inside 90 days.
  • I made the reconciler abort without writing if the call-tracking API errors, because an outage must never be recorded as no calls found.
  • I rejected stamping click IDs on every unattributed win: of 823, 566 never appear in call tracking and 211 of those are repeat buyers, which would teach bidding that ads drive repeat purchases.
  • Double-count suppression is a flag the flow honors, since blanking the field alone gets re-stamped on the next save.

How the result was checked

I read Salesforce's own OAuth usage records instead of taking the import on trust: Google's connector reached 201 uses and is pulling daily. A 22-check stress test creates a throwaway $0 opportunity, runs every flow branch, proves an outage aborts without writing, proves a second run changes nothing, and deletes its test record. The backfill matched the formula field on 740 of 740 records.
