Case studies / Quote-to-cash automation

One click from quote to paid, booked and shipped

Every quote and every paid order was assembled and re-keyed by hand across Salesforce, QuickBooks and ShipStation. I built a sales console where one click sends an AI-drafted quote and one click charges the card, books the receipt, creates the shipping order and closes the deal, and 518 quotes and 228 orders went through it.

A B2B industrial equipment company selling by web, phone and dealers, where reps quote by phone and buyers pay by card, purchase order or net terms. I built and run the console and also sell through it as a rep. Designed, built and maintain the console in production, and sell through it as a rep.

The problem

A rep built each quote PDF by hand, wrote the email, looked up shipping and hunted for brochures, then re-keyed paid orders into QuickBooks and ShipStation. Net 30 and purchase-order buyers could not be handled in any tool at all. Underneath, QuickBooks was reusing document numbers across invoices and receipts, with 521 collisions, every one a different customer, so any tool that keyed on the number could bind one customer's order to another's paperwork. Product copy was a second trap: an audit found 83% spec divergence between the QuickBooks and Salesforce descriptions.

What I built

I built one web app with two modes. Send Quote has Claude draft the email from the customer's most recent call transcript, generates a branded PDF, attaches the right brochures, adds an optional Stripe payment link, sends through Salesforce and archives the PDF on the quote and opportunity. Place Order charges the card through Stripe, creates the QuickBooks receipt and the ShipStation order, and emails the receipt. Product copy comes from Salesforce only, and descriptions a rep types flow back into the catalog.

Then I added the way industrial buyers actually pay. An invoice tab creates Net 15, 30 or 60 invoices and ships on creation. A purchase-order reader runs the buyer's PO through Claude with schema-enforced output and fills only blank fields, transcribing verbatim because the buyer's own spelling is what their receiving dock matches on. A prepay mode holds the order and a sweep driven off QuickBooks payments releases it. One global document sequence now spans invoices and receipts.

Finally I closed every path by which one sale could become two. Charges carry an idempotency key built from the attempt plus a hash of the money fields, the quote path reuses an open opportunity through a scored matcher, QuickBooks retries look for a record already written before cutting another, and POST requests are never auto-retried.

Decisions that mattered

  • I reversed my own shipped behavior so net-terms invoices no longer close the opportunity at send, because nothing downstream told won from paid and a rep could be credited up to 30 days before the money arrived.
  • Timeouts on a write are treated as unknown, never failed, because the record may exist and only the response was lost.
  • I declined to regenerate 417 pre-fix PDFs across 357 quotes, because a rebuild could disagree on price with what the customer received.
  • Anything the warehouse must act on rides as a line item or printed field, never a note.

How the result was checked

Each fix carries a regression test named for the incident that caused it, and the invoice suite runs 170/170. Two proposed quote-recall filters were measured before shipping and reverted because they would have hidden 464 real quotes. The shipping-method table is guarded by 33 assertions, and the shipping-line backfill was checked against QuickBooks order by order.
